Who he is

  • Jason Abbott is the founder and CEO of Oi Furniture. He is based in Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada (his LinkedIn profile).
  • His LinkedIn headline calls him a workspace builder and analytics innovator, and he also lists himself as co-founder of Max Analytics (his LinkedIn profile).
  • He is a serial founder. He has started or co-founded five companies, including Oi Furniture, Catapult Consulting Group, Launch Coworking and Max Evaluation & Analytics (his GCUC coworking conference attendee page).
  • Earlier in his career he worked at UPS Canada and then at Cocoon Branding, where he was VP of business development (2014 University of Winnipeg Leadership Symposium bio; his LinkedIn profile).

The business

  • Oi Furniture makes soft, modular seating called Cellular. The pieces snap together like Lego, and staff can rearrange them into new layouts without tools (ilikeoi.com).
  • He has run Oi since 2008 (his LinkedIn profile). The website prices layouts from $1,774 for one armchair to about $5,000 and up for large bench and lounge layouts (ilikeoi.com).
  • Oi sells online and through authorized resellers, and the site has a "Become a dealer" page, so dealers are part of how it sells (ilikeoi.com).

Who they sell to

  • Schools and libraries.
  • Children's museums and family entertainment centres.
  • Offices, coworking spaces and homes.
  • Retail and hospitality spaces.
  • All four groups are listed as the main "Environments" on ilikeoi.com.

Problem they solve

  • Their buyers want seating that is flexible, tough and fun, and that their own staff can change without tools or a contractor.
  • Schools and libraries want a space students actually use and that still looks good years later. The reviews on the site make exactly that point (ilikeoi.com reviews).

What's interesting for this call

  • He booked on Nov 24 from the webinar chat link, so he saw the webinar live. On the form he picked a deal size of "3-10K" per year and said yes to "interested in AI automation." Ask what the 3-10K means to him: one order, or a whole year of sales, and what he hopes AI automation would fix.
  • His buyers are schools, libraries and offices, and schools usually buy slowly through budgets and procurement. Ask how many new institutions he reaches each month, who signs the order, and how long a sale takes.
  • He sells direct and through dealers. Ask which channel brings most new orders today, and which one he wants to grow.
  • He knows marketing well. He ran a marketing consultancy for founders and led the Manitoba chapter of the Canadian Marketing Association for years (2014 University of Winnipeg Leadership Symposium bio). Expect him to test the details, so be specific about how the webinar system works.

Rapport (personal)
