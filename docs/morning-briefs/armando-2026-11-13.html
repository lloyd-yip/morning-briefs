Who he is

  • John Winner is CEO and co-founder of Kizen, based in Austin, Texas. GHL only has his first name, but his email (john@kizen.com) and LinkedIn match John Winner.
  • Kizen is the sixth company he has founded. He started his first business at 13, setting up and repairing home computers in Palm Beach Gardens, Florida.
  • He graduated from the University of Texas at Austin in 2011 and ran a management consultancy focused on data, automation and AI before starting Kizen in 2018.

The business

  • Kizen sells what it calls an "Agentic Orchestration System" that safely puts AI into enterprise workflows. Products include an AI-native CRM and several AI "CoPilots" for contracting, commissions, marketing and underwriting.
  • The site shows outcome claims like 80% less manual time per loan, 74% faster agent onboarding, and four times more contracts per team member.
  • They raised a $12M seed round, and in 2022 they said they had sold more than 400,000 user licenses.
  • Every page pushes "Book a demo." There is no free trial, newsletter or lead magnet.

Who they sell to

  • Enterprises in regulated industries: insurance carriers and agencies, banks, credit unions, mortgage lenders, and healthcare organizations.
  • The buyers are operations, distribution and technology leaders who run heavy manual workflows like onboarding agents, contracting and loan processing.

Problem they solve

  • Regulated companies want AI, but their data sits in many disconnected systems and they cannot risk an AI tool that breaks compliance.
  • Kizen connects that data and automates the manual steps, so teams get more done without adding headcount.

What’s interesting for this call

  • He came in from a paid Facebook ad and filled out the application before booking the strategy session. He did not come from a webinar, so walk him through the method from the start.
  • He sells high-ticket enterprise deals with a "Book a demo" button as the only way in. Ask how many demos a month they get today, where they come from, and what one new enterprise customer is worth to them.
  • He is a sixth-time founder and ran a consultancy, so expect sharp questions on numbers and process. Be crisp and lead with outcomes.

Rapport (personal)

  • He named the company after the Japanese idea of kaizen (continuous improvement), which he first learned about at UT Austin in 2009, and he bought the domain from a man who called himself a prince in the Philippines (Kizen newsroom, "Kizen Lands $12 Million in Funding").
  • The Wall Street Journal once added "yes, that's his real name" when writing about him, which makes for an easy laugh if the moment is right (Wall Street Journal profile).
