Asif Iqbal · Juspay · Call 1 · Fri Oct 30, 2026, 9:00 AM ET · Edwin

Who he is

  • Asif leads US market expansion for Juspay, focused on getting American merchants to adopt Juspay Hyperswitch. He has been in that seat since January 2025 and is based in Austin, Texas.
  • Before Juspay he spent years at HP, first as a technical program manager in e-commerce and then as Principal for Consumer Subscriptions go-to-market. Earlier he was a project manager at TEKsystems.
  • He describes himself as having more than 13 years in payments, e-commerce and subscription platforms.
  • He is a business development leader at a big company, not the owner. Find out early who signs off on a marketing spend like ours.

The business

  • Juspay is a large payments infrastructure company, roughly 1,000 to 2,000 people, founded in 2012, with about $198M raised and offices in India, Singapore, Dublin, São Paulo and San Mateo.
  • His product, Hyperswitch, is an open-source payments platform. It lets a merchant plug in several payment providers, route transactions, run checkout, store cards safely and reconcile payments, without getting locked into one vendor.
  • They pitch it as "Linux for payments." Merchants can host it themselves or use it as a hosted service.

Who they sell to

  • Mid-size and enterprise merchants and marketplaces in the US and Canada, especially ones selling across several countries or running subscriptions.
  • The buyer is usually a head of payments, a payments product manager or an engineering lead who is tired of costly, rigid payment setups.

Problem they solve

  • Every new payment provider can take months of developer work, fees stack up, and failed payments quietly cost revenue.
  • Hyperswitch lets a merchant add providers through settings instead of code and route each payment to the cheapest or most reliable option.

What’s interesting for this call

  • On his booking form he picked B2B SaaS, said his main problem is not getting qualified leads, and said he wants to start immediately. Ask what "qualified" means to him and what pipeline number he is carrying for the US.
  • He came in through our paid ads, not a webinar, so he has not seen the method yet. Keep the first part of the call about his problem before showing anything.
  • He has said publicly that the best payments conversations start with merchant problems, not products. A webinar built around a merchant pain, like "why your payment costs keep rising," fits how he already likes to sell.
  • Because Juspay is big, ask how US marketing decisions get made, what budget his team controls, and who else would need to be on a second call.

Rapport (personal)

  • He moved from consumer subscriptions at HP to open-source payments infrastructure, so asking what pulled him from a big corporate role into building a new market is an easy, warm start (LinkedIn).
  • He has posted about Juspay expanding into Brazil and Latin America as exciting news, which gives you a friendly "how's that going" question (LinkedIn).
